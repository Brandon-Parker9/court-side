🏐 Volleyball basics

The short version, if you need a refresher.
  • Two teams of 6 play at a time — squads run up to 8, with subs filling the extra spots.
  • Every serve ends in a point for someone (rally scoring). First team to 25, winning by at least 2, takes the set. A deciding set is played to 15, still win by 2.
  • Court positions are numbered 1–6. A team only rotates — everyone shifts one spot clockwise — when they win the serve back from the other team (a "side-out"). Winning a rally while already serving just keeps the same order.
  • Position 1 (back right) always serves.
  • Each team gets up to 3 touches to send the ball back over — commonly dig (or receive) → set → attack.
  • A ball touched by a blocker doesn't count toward a team's 3 touches, and a block that deflects out of bounds is scored against the blocking team, not the hitter.
  • Common faults: hitting the ball into the net or out of bounds, touching the net during play, a double contact or a "carry" (holding/scooping the ball instead of a clean hit), and foot faults on the serve.

📏 More rules worth knowing

Official indoor rules — rec leagues often relax a few of these.
  • Court and net — the court is 18 m × 9 m, with an attack line 3 m from the net on each side. Official nets are 2.60 m for men and 2.24 m for women; rec and co-ed leagues vary (the 3D court uses 2.35 m).
  • Serving — serve from behind the end line within 8 seconds of the whistle. Toss or release the ball and hit it with one hand or arm. A serve that clips the net and still goes over is in play.
  • In or out — a ball touching a line is in. It's out if it lands outside the lines, hits an antenna or the net outside the antennae, crosses the net outside the antennae, or touches the ceiling or anything off the court (some gyms play low ceilings as a house rule).
  • Positions at the serve — when the ball is served, each player has to be in rotation order with their neighbours (front/back and left/right), or it's an overlap fault. Once it's served, everyone can move anywhere.
  • Clean contacts — you can play the ball off any part of your body, but it has to be hit, not caught or thrown. No one may touch it twice in a row, except after a block touch, or when a hard ball hits a player twice within one action on the team's first contact.
  • At the net — touching the net between the antennae while playing the ball is a fault. Crossing the centre line is only a fault if your whole foot lands in the other court.
  • Back row — back-row players can attack from behind the attack line, but can't block, and can't attack a ball that's entirely above the net from in front of it. Nobody may block the serve.
  • The libero — a back-row specialist in a different-coloured jersey who can't block or attack a ball entirely above the net, and swaps in and out without using a substitution. Some leagues let the libero serve in one rotation. In this app, "Libero" is simply the name of position 6, which rotates like every other spot.
  • Subs, timeouts and sides — official play allows 6 substitutions per set, and a starter who comes off can only return to their own spot. Each team gets 2 timeouts of 30 seconds per set. Teams switch ends after each set, and at 8 points in a deciding set.

🎲 What can happen in a rally (Simulation)

The Simulation models a lot more than just "kill or error".
  • Serve — an ace, a return, or a miss. Low serve skill misses a lot — mostly short serves that don't clear the net — while a strong server rarely misses at all. A sharp server also aims at the weaker passer.
  • Passing quality — every receive, dig, and scramble is rated Perfect / Good / Poor / Overpass / Shank rather than just succeeding automatically. A shank costs the point immediately; a poor pass drags down whatever comes next.
  • Overpass — a pass that sails right back over the net untouched, handing the other team an easy, mostly-undefended swing.
  • Real 3-touch limit — each side gets receive/dig → set → attack. A bad enough set can force a team to burn their last touch just saving it, leading to either a scrambled free ball over the net or an outright four hits fault if someone tries to attack anyway. A double contact or carry on the set can also draw the whistle.
  • Attack type — most swings are hard-driven, but a hitter will sometimes tip it softly, go for a roll shot, or the setter will sneak in a surprise dump.
  • Blocking — the blocker is the player across the net from the hitter, sometimes helped by the middle. A block can stuff the attack outright, deflect it out for a point to the attacker, or just stay alive as a scramble for the hitting team to dig and try again.
  • Rare stuff happens too: a blocker can touch the net (automatic point to the attacker), a hit can go wide of the antenna, or the ball can clip the ceiling.
  • Opponent skill — before starting, a 1–10 slider sets how good the other team is overall. Each opponent's serve/attack/receive varies a little around that level, so they don't all play identically.
  • Set stats — open Set stats under the match controls for a box score: side-out %, aces, kills, hitting percentage, blocks, digs, pass rating and faults. It opens by itself when the set ends.

🔄 How subs work here

A simplified model built for pickup and rec games.
  • With 7 or 8 players, the extras sub in through a side you choose — left or right.
  • A right-side sub enters straight into the serving spot (Position 1) the moment it's their turn.
  • A left-side sub enters one step further around, at Position 4 — after passing through Positions 6 and 5.
  • Whoever gets bumped off court waits on that same side's bench until it's their turn to come back in.
  • Each bench is a line: the player marked next in comes on first. Drag bench players to reorder them, or choose who's next in under By position. With one sub on each side, both come in on the first rotation.
  • If a starter can't make it, the next sub covers their spot. Add a fill-in for them (or check them back in) and everything goes back to how it was.
  • The coach sheet marks the current rotation and shows who comes on for whom at the next rotation.

🎥 The 3D court

Switch the Simulation from 2D to 3D to watch the set on a full court.
  • Moving around — drag to look around, pinch or scroll to zoom from close up to 26 m out, and tap a player (or their name tag) to watch from where they stand. Back to court view returns you. Until you touch the view it slowly circles the court.
  • Cameras — Broadcast, Sideline, Overhead and End line jump to a fixed view; dragging or zooming frees the camera again.
  • Reading the court — your team wears gold, opponents blue. Jersey numbers follow your roster order. A white ring marks the player making the current play, a gold ring the player waiting to serve, and a green L tag whoever is in the Libero spot.
  • What the effects mean — the ball has a soft glow so it's easy to follow. A short streak trails fast serves and spikes, hard contacts flash, the net bulges when the ball or a blocker hits it, and an antenna wobbles when it's clipped. Players move with the play: the next player to touch the ball starts moving early, and every touch is timed so hands meet the ball.
  • Replay point — watch the last finished rally again at half speed. The match stays paused; Stop replay or Resume ends it.
  • Details — shows what the simulation decided for each play (pass quality, attack type, result), where the ball is heading, each team's formation, and the current graphics quality.
  • Loading and speed — the 3D court needs an internet connection the first time it loads; offline, or without WebGL, the 2D court is used. On slower devices it lowers the resolution and then turns off real-time shadows automatically.

📱 Using this app

  • Roster — add up to 8 players and set each player's serve, attack, and receive skill. Import FireWalls fills an empty roster; if your roster is already FireWalls, it only adds whoever's missing and keeps everyone's skills.
  • Lineup — check off who showed up (Select everyone checks the whole roster in one tap) and add a same-day replacement if someone can't make it — they take that player's spot. Then build the starting lineup on the court: drag a player onto another spot or a bench to swap them (on a phone, press and hold first), or tap one player and then where they should go. Shuffle makes a random lineup (with Undo), and Set positions lets you pick By position (who plays each spot) or By player (each player's spot). Step through the rotations to preview them.
  • Sheet — from the bottom of Lineup, view every rotation at once, or print it for whoever's running subs court-side.
  • Simulation — pick the opponent's squad size, skill level, and playback speed, then watch a full set play out point-by-point using your players' actual skills and the same rotation rules as your real lineup. The status line under the heading always says whether the match is ready, playing, paused, replaying or final.
